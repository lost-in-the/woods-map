Priority scoring

four criteria, 0–3 each, summed
S size
how much of the page it covers and how many pages it is on. 3 = every page or the main column, 2 = most pages or a section, 1 = one board or a corner, 0 = incidental
C colour
how much meaning rides on colour. 3 = colour is the meaning (chips, legends, diagram classes), 2 = colour marks state (selection, buttons, links), 1 = tinted but readable without it, 0 = grayscale
K contrast
accessibility exposure. 3 = small text in gray, or colour-only meaning, 2 = 12px text in slate or a colour pair to check, 1 = body text on a surface, 0 = none
D division
how much it structures content. 3 = defines boundaries readers scan by (tables, section headers, panels), 2 = groups within a boundary, 1 = separates a line, 0 = inline
score
S + C + K + D, out of 12. Ties break on S, then name
P1 ≥ 9
define first: a specimen with states, tokens, spacing, and the accessibility note. Nothing else is built until P1 is agreed
P2 6–8
define next; most reuse P1 tokens and only need the variant written down
P3 ≤ 5
inherit: a row in a table on this page, no specimen unless a board drifts
foundations
not scored. Palette, type scale, spacing scale, borders, text overflow, tooltips, dates and reading order come before any component, since every component is built from them

Colour vision and light mode

simulated with the Machado 2009 matrices at full severity; ΔE is CIE76 in Lab, contrast is WCAG on the mode’s background
The three warm statuses were the problem. In dark mode orange #E66747 and beak #E64D47 are 13 ΔE apart for normal vision and 4.7 for deuteranopia: the same colour. Lifting orange to #EF8A3C (same family, more sun in it, a 12° hue move your note allows) takes that to 35 / 19 and adds 12 L* of lightness between them, so even a monochrome reading separates sun, modified and risk. Light mode cannot do the same trick, because darkened warm colours converge; there the separation comes from lightness and from the glyph each status always carries. GitHub’s Primer reaches the same conclusion: every status has an icon, and colour-blind users get a theme where “modified” borrows the blue hue. The map does both.

Light mode uses the palette’s unlifted purple, cobalt and forest unchanged: they were designed for white and pass AA there. The warm three are darkened to pass; under deuteranopia they converge (ΔE 7–13), so in light mode the glyph is the signal and colour is the confirmation.
Colour never alonemodified always shows M, hot always the word hot, risk always ⚑ or a badge word, fresh the ● dot with “current”, selected the 3px bar and the checkbox state. Remove every colour from a board and the meaning survives; that is the test each specimen passes.
Colour-vision themea user setting, same as the light switch: modified borrows the link hue (cobalt+ in dark, cobalt in light), links become always-underlined so the two never collide, hot stays sun, risk stays beak. Nothing else changes; the tokens make it a four-line override. Primer ships the same idea as dark_colorblind.
What GitHub’s guides addtheir brand accessibility page sets one rule the map keeps: never only colour for meaning, and charts never distinguish series by colour alone. Their product palette (Primer) separates attention / severe / danger by hue and lightness and pairs each with an icon, which is the shape the map now has. The brand palette itself (GitHub green, five percent accents over neutrals) is a proportion rule: mostly neutral, colour as the exception, which rule 9 already says.

Rules from your notes, as decided

your answers from the comment threads folded in; the one line at the bottom of each card is the decision
1Relative datessettled
  • Git’s relative-date algorithm, unchanged: its unit boundaries (seconds → minutes → hours → days → weeks → months → years) and its rounding, so the map shows what git log --date=relative would, with the unit abbreviated and nothing else reformatted.
  • Units: s m h d w mo y, glued to the integer: 3d, 5w, 7mo, 1y. m is minutes because git has minutes; months take mo.
  • Git’s two-unit form past a year (“1 year, 2 months”) becomes 1y 2mo.
  • Absolute timestamp in the tooltip and the accessible name.
git’s algorithm, units abbreviated, no reformatting of the value
2Spacing on a golden scalesettled
  • Scale: 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55 px (Fibonacci, each step ≈ φ × the last).
  • Inline gaps 5 and 8; cell padding 8 × 13; section gap 21; panel padding 13 × 21; page gutter 34; between page regions 55.
  • Row height follows from 8 + line + 8, never set directly.
  • Current boards use 4, 6, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24: a mechanical pass maps each to the nearest step.
35813213455
starts at 3
3Borders, widths, radiisettled
  • One width: 1px, colour rule (dusk) for every divider, cell, column rule and panel edge.
  • rule2 only on things you can press: buttons, inputs, kbd, the outer table frame; the row toggle is a bare + / − with no box.
  • 2px is reserved for meaning: the process boundary in steps, the active tab underline.
  • Radius 3px on controls and panels, 2px on chips, 0 on tables and cells.
  • Colour on a border always means something: purple+ = selected, sun = attention, beak = risk. Nothing else gets a coloured border.
the 3px selection bar is the one exception; a new case gets raised before it is added
4Heading hierarchysettled
  • h1 page title, 24px bold snow, sans; the subtitle beside it is slate 15px mono (it is data: unit type, table, author).
  • h2 section, 18px bold snow, sans, with its count in slate mono and a rule under the whole header row; the rule is what says “this table belongs to me”. ⓘ as a suffix when the section needs explaining.
  • h3 rail and tile eyebrows and panel header bands, 11px uppercase slate sans. Active rail heading purple+.
  • th is not a heading: 11px uppercase slate, panel background.
  • Nothing skips a level; every table and diagram sits under an h2 or h3 that names it, and aria-labelledby points at it.
h2 at 18px; two typefaces carry the rest of the differentiation
5Word breaks and overflowsettled
  • Identifiers never break: white-space:nowrap on names, paths, SHAs, routes, methods. Tables scroll sideways instead, like an editor.
  • Prose cells wrap at words only; no anywhere, no hyphenation.
  • Ellipsis is a smell: shorten first (shorthand, drop the namespace, keep the tail of a path); where it stays, the full text is in title and the accessible name.
  • Shorthand before abbreviation: gen for generation, deps, refs; nothing from the abbreviations-in-code list that a Rails developer would not already read.
  • Dotted underline + tooltip on product shorthand (gen, refs, cov); none on standard notation (PK, FK, UK, SHA, LOC, ERD).
shorthand over abbreviation, underline only what is ours
6Tooltips and hint textsettled
  • Tooltip when: an icon-only control, product shorthand, a truncated value, a number whose basis is not shown (what counts as hot), an absolute date behind a relative one.
  • Never a tooltip for: anything needed to act, anything longer than a sentence, keyboard shortcuts (status bar owns those).
  • Explanations move to a ⓘ suffix on the section header; the caption under a table keeps provenance only (source, generation, what is not shown and where it is).
  • Legends stay: they decode glyphs.
  • One tooltip component: 12px sans, panel background, rule2 border, a caret pointing at its anchor, 5 × 8 padding, below and left-aligned (above when there is no room), 300ms delay, also on focus; it may hold a link, so it stays open while hovered or focused and is reachable with Tab; on touch it opens on tap and closes on a tap outside.
ⓘ on headers, as a suffix
7Colour meaning across viewssettled
  • Purple+ #B580BC replaces #A78BFA and means selected or active everywhere: checked rows, active rail heading, focus ring, chosen segment, the one primary button.
  • Warm hues order by severity, increasing redness: sun #F1B73A = modified (the M chip, changed diff rows, the changed-box tint), orange′ #EF8A3C = hot and warnings, beak #E64D47 = risk. forest+ = fresh and added; cobalt+ = links. Your revision of 2026-10-16 overrides the editor-theme swap; the colour-vision section still applies to orange′.
  • Tints: 10% orange behind a changed box in diagrams, 8% purple+ behind a checked row, dusk behind a hovered row; saturation steps are allowed, hue shifts are not.
  • Buttons are boxes, links are cobalt+ text, tools are slate text with a rule2 underline; a thing is one of the three.
purple+ and cobalt+ adopted; sun modified, orange′ hot, beak risk: redder is worse
8Subdivided rowssettled
  • A row subdivides when one item has facts that are not columns: files of a commit, calls and effects of a callback, candidates of an ambiguous edge.
  • Two shapes only: the detail block (label column + values under the row, divider above, a bare + / − on the row) and the numbered sub-table for ordered content.
  • Never a third shape; if content fits neither, it is a column or its own table.
  • Group-parity striping, the item’s rows share one band; a detail block is collapsible, a sub-table is not.
closed by default, with “expand all” in the section header; skimming wins
9Reading order and weightprinciples
  • Anchor each page with the numbers that let a reader stop: facts tiles first; if they look normal, the tables below are skippable.
  • One layout, one concept: every section is header → picture → table → caption, so the eye finds the same evaluation point each time.
  • Takeaway headers: the count after an h2 carries the verdict (“Risks 2, 1 blocking”), not just the noun.
  • Bold the champion column (the identifier), dim the secondary (dates, SHAs, layer).
  • Progressive disclosure: 5 rows and “all 23”, detail blocks closed, exact values in tooltips.
  • Colour only for anomalies; a section with no colour reads as nominal. Sticky section headers on long pages, which the rail already provides.
guidance, not hard rules; the per-page pass will test them
